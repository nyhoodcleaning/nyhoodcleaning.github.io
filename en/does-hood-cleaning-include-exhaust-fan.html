“Hood cleaning” does not always describe the entire job

A restaurant exhaust system includes more than the visible hood above the cooking equipment. When reviewing a quote, look for the actual components listed rather than relying only on the service name.

A basic package may focus on the hood and a limited area of accessible ductwork. Filters, exhaust fans and additional equipment may be separate items. Ask the provider to explain the proposed scope before you approve the work.

Hood and filter areas

Confirm which interior surfaces are included and whether the filters will be cleaned as part of the service.

Accessible ductwork

Ask which sections can be reached and whether there are access limitations that require further assessment.

Exhaust fan

Check whether the fan is listed in the quote and identify how many units are included.

Heavy grease removal

Describe thick or hardened deposits so the provider can account for the additional work involved.

Specialized tools for thick, stubborn grease

Some kitchen exhaust surfaces need more than a simple wipe-down. Thick, built-up grease can require targeted manual removal before the remaining residue is cleaned.

We have specialized grease-scraping tools

Our team uses specialized scraping tools to remove thick, stubborn grease deposits from suitable, accessible hood, duct and exhaust fan surfaces. We select the tools and cleaning method according to the equipment, surface condition and access.

Where appropriate, manual scraping is followed by degreasing and cleaning with our professional equipment. The approach is chosen for the component being cleaned; not every surface or part should be treated the same way.

Tell us if your kitchen has gone a long time without professional cleaning or if you can see thick grease deposits. That information helps us assess the work before confirming the quote.

How we assess the cleaning work

Best Commercial Hood Cleaning considers the equipment and its condition before confirming the service. The goal is to make the cleaning scope understandable and match the work to your kitchen.

  1. Identify the requested equipment. We discuss the hood, filters, accessible duct sections and any fan cleaning you want included.
  2. Review grease condition. Available photos and service history help us understand whether thick deposits or additional manual scraping may be involved.
  3. Confirm access. Equipment layout and access arrangements affect what can be assessed and cleaned.
  4. Agree on the scope. We discuss included components, separately priced work and whether an on-site assessment is needed.

Cleaning methods must suit the equipment. Scraping and washing are professional service tasks, carried out with appropriate equipment shutdown and protection of sensitive components—not work to attempt on a running fan.

Five questions to ask before booking

  • Is exhaust fan cleaning included in this price? Ask for it to be clearly listed if you want it included.
  • How many fans and hood sections does the quote cover? Make sure the equipment count matches your kitchen.
  • Does the quote account for heavy grease scraping? Discuss thick deposits and any conditions that could change the scope or price.
  • Are there areas that cannot be reached? Understand access limitations before the appointment.
  • What records will be provided after service? Ask how completed work and any remaining limitations will be documented.

Compare the same work. A lower-priced quote may cover fewer components. Comparing itemized scopes helps you understand the difference between the offers.

What to send for a restaurant cleaning quote

Send a few details so we can discuss your service more efficiently:

  • Restaurant address and contact information
  • Approximate hood length and filter count
  • Last professional cleaning date, if known
  • Photos of visible grease and accessible equipment
  • Whether exhaust fan cleaning is requested
  • Known access arrangements or equipment issues
  • Preferred service time

Existing fan photos can be helpful, but you do not need to climb onto the roof or open equipment to obtain them. If the available information is insufficient, we can discuss an on-site assessment.

Exhaust fan cleaning: common questions

Is exhaust fan cleaning included in every hood cleaning package?

No. It depends on the agreed package and quote. Tell us you want fan cleaning included so we can assess the equipment and list it in the scope.

Do you have tools to scrape off heavy grease?

Yes. We use specialized grease-scraping tools to remove thick deposits from suitable, accessible surfaces. Tool selection and the cleaning method depend on the equipment and its condition.

Can you clean a system with years of grease buildup?

Send photos and describe its cleaning history. Heavy buildup may require additional manual work and an on-site assessment before the scope and price can be confirmed.

Will cleaning repair a fan that is not working?

Cleaning is not a substitute for mechanical or electrical repairs. Report any operating problems when you contact us so the appropriate service can be discussed.
